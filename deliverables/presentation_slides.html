Problem Definition & Motivation

The Ground Motion Directionality Problem

Horizontal ground shaking varies with azimuth. The maximum horizontal spectral acceleration is known as RotD100.

⚠️ Current Industry Assumption

Standard building codes (ASCE 7-16) and regional risk assessments treat directionality orientations across sites as independent uniform distributions over [0°, 180°).

Flaw: In reality, nearby sites (< 5 km) experience coherent polarized seismic wavefields. Assuming independence drastically underestimates simultaneous failure of distributed infrastructure (bridges, pipelines, power grids).

🎯 Project Objective

Develop supervised ML models to predict the acute angular difference (Δθ ∈ [0°, 90°]) in direction of maximum shaking between any two sites si and sj during earthquake Ek:

Δθ(si, sj, Ek) = f(si, sj, Ek)

Data Engineering

NGA-West2 Database & Pairwise Construction

1. Event Filtering

Filtered shallow crustal records for engineering significance: Mw ≥ 5.0, retaining ~5,000 global records.

2. Spatial Pairing

Paired all stations recording the same earthquake with inter-site distance d ≤ 5.0 km, generating 5,300 pairs.

3. Ground Truth Target

Computed RotD100 orientations from waveforms: Δθ = min(|θi - θj|, 180° - |θi - θj|).

📊 Initial Feature Pool (71 Candidate Attributes)

• Earthquake Source: Mw, focal mechanism, strike, dip, rake, rupture area/length/width, hypocenter depth, ZTOR.
• Dual-Site Geometries: Repi, Rhyp, Rjb, Rrup, inter-site Euclidean distance d.
• Geotechnical Soil: Shear wave velocity Vs30, NEHRP classes B–E, impedance ratio.
• Intensity Metrics: Peak Ground Acceleration (PGA), PGV, PGD at each station & paired averages.

Feature Selection

Stepwise Backward-Forward Analysis

To eliminate multicollinearity and prevent overfitting on 71 features, an iterative Ridge-based selection was deployed:

⏪ Stage 1: Backward Ranking

Ranks each feature by the drop in validation R2 when removed from the full model:
ΔR² = R²base - R²without_i

Key Result: Inter-site separation distance d and average PGA exhibited the largest drop, confirming their physical dominance.

⏩ Stage 2: Forward Selection

Sequentially adds features in ranked order until validation R2 plateaus/decreases due to overfitting.

Optimal Subset: Selected 21 features including distance d, average PGA, Vs30 mismatch, rupture length, and radiation factors.

Model Architecture

Benchmarked Machine Learning Models

1. Baseline Benchmark

Simple Univariate OLS:
Δθ = c₁ + c₂ · d

Uses only inter-site distance d. Serves as reference for spatial decay.

2. Ridge Regression (L2)

Regularized Linear Model:
min ||y - Xβ||² + α||β||²

Tuned on 21 selected features with optimal α = 1.5 via validation search.

3. Deep Neural Net (MLP)

Non-Linear Architecture:
Deep MLP with ReLU activations, Adam optimizer, and weight decay α = 0.1.

🔍 Hyperparameter Grid Search (Matching Paper Figure 8)

Explored depths from 1 to 5 hidden layers and widths from 50 to 400 neurons. Multi-layer topologies capture complex non-linear radiation directivity and site impedance contrasts.

Physics & Discussion

Physical Interpretation & Aleatoric Uncertainty

🌊 Physical Wave Trends

• Distance Decay: At small separation (d < 1 km), wavefields are coherent (Δθ ≈ 15°–20°). By d = 5 km, scattering decorrelates shaking toward uniform randomness (Δθ ≈ 45°).

• PGA Polarization: Strong shaking (> 0.15g) is source-dominated, aligning ground motion with fault rupture and reducing Δθ.

🎲 Inherent Aleatoric Uncertainty

Why is the maximum test R2 bounded around ~0.35–0.40?

Ground shaking directionality has large aleatoric (irreducible) uncertainty from 3D basin geometry, scattering, and velocity heterogeneity not measurable from surface station metadata.

Crucial Success: While individual pair scatter is high, the model's moving average perfectly tracks true ground motion physics.
